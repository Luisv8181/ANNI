## Decisions (aligned)
1. **Case quantity — 5.** Build the initial synthetic profile from **5 high-quality annotated cases**;
   run a follow-up comparing **5 vs 10** to see whether more testimony helps.
2. **Outcome variability — open + closed.** Model the same patient on two paths: an **open** outcome
   (free to improve or not, mimicking a real client) and a **closed** predetermined **"treatment-failure"**
   outcome. Tracking both shows the *path* to failure vs. success. (Grounded in research that ~5–10% of
   GAD patients deteriorate after starting therapy.)
3. **Annotation ethics — inspiration, not replication.** Extract **common characteristics** across
   testimonies and **cite** them; never reproduce a specific person's story. "Be inspired by it, don't be it."
4. **Blinding.** The synthetic patient is **not told it's talking to an AI**. Knowing-vs-not-knowing is a
   later-phase comparison; the initial phase stays blind to preserve integrity.
5. **DSM-5 baseline.** Program **DSM-5 GAD** criteria as the patient's clinical baseline, then layer
   testimony-derived natural human characteristics on top.

## Design points to build
- **Patient self-consistency reviewer.** Like the therapist's self-assessment, the synthetic patient
  needs an internal check that it stays in character and doesn't lose context / make things up over long
  runs. Open question: does forcing consistency make the patient *resistant* to legitimate change from
  the therapist? Plan: allow a trial window (e.g., first ~20–30 turns) where behavior is still tunable,
  then lock the system prompt.
- **Turns & stages.** Start at **~20 turns/case**; also test **long runs of 70–75 turns** to see if the
  patient (and therapist) retain earlier context. Run **multiple sessions per persona** at different
  **stages** of the counseling life cycle (beginning / middle / later).
- **Relay lab (blind).** Copy both sides — Wysa / ChatGPT ↔ the Ollama synthetic patient — into the lab
  so every part "thinks" it's talking to a person. (Relay Mode is in the lab.)
